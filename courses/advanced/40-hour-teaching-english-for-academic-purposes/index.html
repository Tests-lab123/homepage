How it works

Every year, millions of international students apply to universities and colleges in English-speaking countries such as the UK, USA, Canada and Australia. Many of these students don’t speak English as their first language but entry to these institutions and continued success is dependent on an excellent command of the language. 

All this means that English for Academic Purposes (EAP) lessons are highly sought after by students around the world, which makes it a specialised area of TEFL in high demand. 

This advanced TEFL course will take you through everything you need to know to start teaching EAP lessons for the first time. You'll learn about the different types of EAP teaching, the skills most important to students studying at this level, and how to create the most effective lessons for your students. By the end of this course, you’ll be qualified to teach EAP for the first time and start charging for lessons.

Please note: this course assumes prior TEFL knowledge, so we recommend first completing a 120-hour/Level 5 TEFL course.

Unit breakdown

Our 40-hour English for Academic Purposes TEFL Course consists of 5 modules.

    Teaching English for Academic PurposesWhat EAP students need to learnAcademic writingEssential grammar and skills practiceHow to teach EAP lessons

Teaching English for Academic Purposes

This module will introduce you to the field of English for Academic Purposes. You’ll learn what it is, who studies it, about common types of EAP courses, and what makes teaching EAP different from General English.

What EAP students need to learn

Learn all about the language of EAP. Even though each student you teach will be different, they will all have language needs in common, including the vocabulary they need to learn. This module will guide you through teaching general, academic and subject-specific vocabulary, as well as collocations and phrasal verbs.

Academic writing

There are various types of academic writing and essay styles. You will learn about the differences between descriptive, analytical, and expository essays, and cover other forms of writing students may need to use, such as case studies, articles and other communications.

Essential grammar and skills practice

This module is all about grammar. You'll learn about nominalisation and noun phrasing - both essential academic writing features - as well as the passive voice and how to write objectively. You’ll also look at the essential skills of academic reading, listening and speaking as well.

How to teach EAP lessons

Everything you’ve learned so far will come together in this final module. It covers lesson structuring, including PPP and skills lesson structures and when it’s appropriate to use each one, as well as using authentic resources in your lessons. You’ll also learn how to conduct a needs analysis, so your lessons are as effective as possible.
